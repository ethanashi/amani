## 1. The guarantee, restated

**The guarantee a Confirmed label carries.** It replaces the round-1 class-D wording, which is withdrawn:

> *Validated: the end-to-end false-Confirmed rate (procedure + refusal-only diagnostics) has a one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson upper bound ≤ 6.25% (two-sided claims) or a false-direction bound ≤ 5% (directional claims) over the listed calibration generators (body-skew parameter ≤ 1, variance ratio ≤ 9, tails as heavy as t3), lattice outcomes, and the bounded rare-contamination cases, at the listed designs and envelope, for data passing the data-validity diagnostics. For the contamination-bound label, contaminating mass must lie within 3 core-SDs of the population core mean. This population assumption is not checked from the sample; unbounded or farther contamination is not covered.*

The last sentence is the important change. It is the reason this is not a "for all distributions" claim.

### 1.1 Why diagnostics cannot close the gap the audit found

The audit's counterexample is a bounded, zero-skew mixture:
- a tight uniform core;
- outliers at +1 (probability .01) and −2 (probability .0012), which is 173 core-SDs;
- arms 2Z (n = 15) and −Z (n = 60).

| Measurement (two-sided .05 claims) | Value |
|---|---|
| Audit, R | 42.65% |
| Audit, TS | 43.96% |
| K2a, diagnostics off | 43.04% |
| K2a, default diagnostics on | 43.00% (CP upper 43.58%) |
| Datasets refused by the default diagnostics | 56.5% |
| False Confirmed results from datasets in which no outlier was drawn (4,000 nulls) | 99.9% |

The diagnostics remove only the datasets in which an outlier is visible. Those were not the false rejections anyway, because a visible outlier inflates the SE.

**What goes wrong.** A false Confirmed comes from a clean, tight sample whose bulk sits slightly off the true mean. The off-centre bulk is what pays for the unseen outliers. That sample is exactly what genuine low-noise data with a real effect look like.

**Why no sample-based rule can fix it.** A refusal rule computed from the sample cannot distinguish the two without also refusing genuine low-noise data. This is Bahadur–Savage in its operational form: for a mean estimand, no procedure can bound size over a class closed under such contamination. Raising the diagnostic level makes no difference; even at α_d = 0.1 the counterexample still rejects about 41%.

So diagnostics can only help against contamination that is visible in the sample. Two consequences follow:
1. **The label must state the contamination assumption.** The adversarial search (section 4) measures where the boundary lies, as a maximum outlier distance in core-SDs.
2. **Claims that must hold without that assumption need an estimand the data identify.** Options are a rank effect (Brunner–Munzel), a quantile, or a bounded-outcome mean with a contract-declared range. Under the counterexample's laws, the relative effect P(A < B) is not 1/2, so a rank test that rejects is correct for its own estimand.

### 1.2 The diagnostics (refusal-only, amendment 1)

They are evaluated on the deciding data, per vector:
- an arm;
- an arm within a stratum;
- the unit contrasts;
- the internally studentized residuals.

Every diagnostic depends only on within-vector deviations. That makes it invariant to rescaling, shifting and row order, and independent of the tested null, so it is consistent with interval inversion.

**1. Influence concentration (Lindeberg/ESD).**
- The single-observation measure is the Lindeberg share d_(1)²/Σd², which equals G²/(n−1) for the Grubbs statistic G.
- It is generalised to the top k = ⌊n/10⌋ observations by Rosner's generalised ESD.
- A claim is refused when any vector has an ESD outlier at level α_d/m, where m is the number of vectors in the claim (Bonferroni). Under normal data the claim-level refusal rate is therefore about α_d.
- **Default α_d = 0.01.** Measured per-vector refusal under normal data was 0.99–1.07% at n = 5–50 for the nominal 1% level. Liveness designs were refused 0.1–1.4% of the time.

**2. Ties and degeneracy.** A vector is refused when it has fewer than 3 distinct values, or its modal value holds more than 50% of it. This targets lattice-dominated outcomes; binary outcomes belong to the RD procedure. In regression the ties vector is the outcome y itself. Revision 2 read the null-imposed y − b0·x there, so the same data could be refused at one tested null and accepted at another (audit repro: x = 0..19, y = x + 20·I(x ≥ 10) gave a CI [2.209, 2.845] at null 0 but refused null 1). Fixed; refusal status is now tested to be identical at every null for every procedure.

**Where no diagnostics apply.**
- Brunner–Munzel: ranks bound every observation's influence. It refuses complete separation.
- Binary RD: not applicable.

### 1.3 What certification requires of the data pipeline

The diagnostics only refuse, so on a FIXED dataset they can never raise the rejection rate. An analyst who chooses which rows enter the dataset after seeing the outcomes can. The R re-audit measured it: dropping one observation per arm, chosen after looking, from 9/9 uniform null samples down to 8/8 gave **25.62%** false positives at a nominal 5%.

So certification REQUIRES:
- **locked cohorts:** the rows are fixed before the outcome is read;
- **contract-declared exclusions:** any exclusion is a rule written into the contract before the outcome is read.

Neither can be checked from the data. They are an obligation on K1b (contracts) and V7 (investigation runtime).

## 4. Adversarial search (end-to-end, procedure + diagnostics + refusals)

**Search space.** At 14 envelope corners, over:
- mixture type: one-point; one-exp (unbounded); two-zeroskew (the audit shape);
- outlier probability p ∈ {.001, .003, .01, .03, .1};
- outlier distance L ∈ {3, 10, 30, 100, 300} core-SDs;
- arm sign pattern;
- variance ratio, 1 or 9.

**Phases.**
- 3,150 screen cells at 300 nulls each;
- 414 refinement cells at 1,000 nulls;
- 56 confirmations at 20,000 fresh nulls: the maximum per corner and objective, for both a uniform and a normal core.

**What this means.**
- The 3-core-SD result applies only to the 48 listed corners inside the revised envelope and to objectives actually tested at each corner. The table reports a pass only when every selected confirmation is present.
- In the regression ladder, binary exposure designs with fewer than 9 observations in the smaller exposure group are outside this bound; the failing m = 6 and m = 8 rows are retained as evidence, not released corners.
- The population core is the unit-variance uniform distribution (with a normal-core confirmation twin), centered at mean zero. Contamination is the remaining probability mass at the declared point locations. Distance is the greatest absolute contaminant location from the core mean in core-SDs; it is not distance from the observed sample mean.
- The 3-SD bound is an assumption about the population measurement process. It cannot be inferred from the observed data and does not apply to one-exp mixtures, whose contamination is unbounded.

**Contamination-bound label wording.** "Validated over the listed calibration generators and designs at the stated envelope, with a population core centered at its mean and contaminating mass no farther than 3 core standard deviations from that mean; the tested two-point law uses locations +L and −2L and is admitted only when its farthest point is within 3 core SDs. For regression with binary exposure, the smaller exposure group must contain at least 9 units. This assumption is not checked from the sample. Results do not cover unbounded contamination, contaminating mass farther than 3 core SDs, or designs outside the stated procedure envelope."

### 4.4 Consequence for the label

Under the coordinator's structure, the end-to-end certification **cannot be met over rare-outlier mixtures in general**, and no refusal threshold changes that (4.1).

What can be stated honestly:
1. **A validated contamination bound.** "Validated for outcome laws whose contaminating mass, if any, lies within K core-SDs of the bulk." K is taken from 4.3. It is an assumption about the measurement process, not a property of the data, and must be printed as such.
2. **Claims that must hold without that assumption** need an estimand the data identify:
   - the Brunner–Munzel relative effect. It is rank-based, so contamination cannot move the bulk without moving the estimand;
   - a quantile;
   - a bounded-outcome mean with a contract-declared range, using the exact bounded-mean test of amendment 3 (not built in K2a).

**What they cost.**
- Under skewed or t3-tailed calibration laws, the default level refuses a median 15–36% of datasets, and α_d 0.1 refuses 22–67%.
- Under normal data, the claim-level refusal rate is about 1% (liveness designs: 0.1–1.4%).
- The refusals fall on exactly the skewed or heavy-tailed data where a mean claim is least trustworthy.

**Recommendation.** Keep α_d = 0.01. The remaining two-group envelope failures at 0.01 are all at n ≤ 15, inside the stated minimum n. Raising α_d to 0.1 buys the last 14 cells at the price of refusing half of all skewed datasets.

**The limit.** The diagnostics do not touch invisible contamination (section 1.1).

## 9. Gaps

### Not certifiable as specified

1. **End-to-end certification over rare-outlier mixtures is not achievable for any mean-type procedure.**
   - Refusal-only diagnostics cannot detect contamination that is absent from the sample (section 1.1), and the adversarial maxima confirm this at every corner (section 4).
   - What is certifiable is either:
     - a validated contamination bound, stated in the label (section 4.3); or
     - claims on estimands the data identify: ranks, quantiles, or a bounded mean with a contract-declared outcome range.
   - The bounded-mean test that amendment 3 names for ALSFRS-R is not built.

### Not built

2. **Prediction skill and held-out prediction (G9).** No procedure exists, so the held-out liveness design is not reachable. The owner's no-egress rule for held-out Confirmed has to be built into that route.
3. **The rest of the amendment-17 scope.** Missing: logistic score/LR/Firth, survival (weighted log-rank, RMST), grouped-CV performance, pseudobulk feature tests and the bounded-mean ALSFRS-R test.
4. **The p13 two-table ANCOVA liveness design.** There is no generator in the evidence directories.
5. **Bootstrap-t is not extended to CR2, weighted Welch or regression.** Their directional envelopes still pass (5–30 units) without it. A Mammen-weight wild bootstrap would be the regression analogue.
6. **Design-matched calibration (amendment 6).** Not built. Neither is size at multiplicity-adjusted thresholds α/m.

### Calibration coverage

7. **No envelope maps for Brunner–Munzel and the ratio of geometric means.** They have release grids only. Brunner–Munzel fails one cell at 5 vs 10 with variance ratio 9 and t3 tails (6.98%).
8. **The grids are structured, not exhaustive.** Regression size cells and adversarial cells now use X fixed by (n, exposure law, covariates, design label), and confirmations reuse that design. The search remains a grid plus local refinement, not a global optimizer; the bounded ladder covers the named corners, not every possible fixed design.
   - Tails are normal or t3 (t5 is dominated). Skew levels are 0 and 1, and variance ratios 1 and 9.
   - The adversarial search is a grid-plus-refinement search, not a global optimizer.
   - The regression envelope uses 2 fixed designs per (design type, n).
9. **Most cells use B = 999.** Only cells within 0.6 points of a limit were re-run at production B = 9,999 (section 2). Cells further from a limit are unlikely to flip, but this has not been checked.
10. **Brunner–Munzel interval validity.** Its interval was checked for coverage at one design only (section 6). Nulls other than 1/2 are refused rather than supported.
11. **Unmodelled data features.**
    - Serial correlation: only the blocked pre/post AR(1) re-probe.
    - Clustered outcomes beyond strata: K4.
    - Crossover carryover: assumed absent, and the label must say so.

## 10. Method

**Procedures.** The simulator runs the registered @2 procedures' own code:
- canonicalisation;
- null-free content seeds;
- decision rules, with the directional bootstrap-t;
- the diagnostics, recorded at α_d ∈ {off, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1}.

Decide-vs-procedure agreement is unit-tested. Resampling uses B = 999, except the boundary re-runs at B = 9,999. Exhaustive enumeration replaces Monte Carlo for small orbits.

**Release rule.**
- Each size cell gets a 2,000-null screen.
- A cell whose end-to-end CP upper bound exceeds the nominal level on any release test gets 20,000 fresh nulls (new seed), and those decide alone.
- Limits: 6.25% two-sided; 5% false direction for each one-sided .025 test.

**Laws.** Every law is standardized to mean 0 and variance 1, then scaled per arm.
- **Tails:** normal, t5 and t3. The generators are normal with Tukey-g transform, t5 with Tukey-g transform, and g-and-h with h = 1/ν for skewed t3 cells.
- **Skew convention:** the calibration parameter is body skew (Tukey g), at 0 or 1. For normal and t5 tails it equals moment skewness; for skewed t3 the third moment is infinite and moment skewness is undefined. The skewed-t3 cells validate the body-skew generator only, not a moment-skewness ≤ 1 bound. Kelly body skew is listed separately in TABLES §6f.
- **Variance ratio:** up to 9, on either arm.
- **Mixtures:** a unit-variance core plus outliers at L core-SDs. There are three types:
  - one-point (+L);
  - one-exp (+L(1 + Exp), unbounded);
  - two-zeroskew (+L and −2L, with the skewness solved to exactly 0). This reproduces the audit's counterexample: q = .0012078 for p = .01, L = 173.
- **Lattice laws:** Poisson(0.5), Poisson(2), a 5-point ordinal and zero-heavy counts.

**Regression.** Size cells sharing (n, exposure law, covariates, design label) use the same fixed X. Each design is evaluated at its own Bell–McCaffrey df, and the report refuses to aggregate cells if their df values disagree. The evaluated calibration cutoffs are df ≥ 7.8 two-sided and df ≥ 10.2 directional; the separate 3-core-SD contamination label also requires at least 9 units in the smaller binary exposure group.

**Adversarial search.** Over (mixture type, p ∈ {.001, .003, .01, .03, .1}, L ∈ {3, 10, 30, 100, 300}, sign pattern, variance ratio 1 or 9) at 14 corners.
- 300-null screen;
- local 3 × 3 refinement of the top 3 per objective at 1,000 nulls;
- 20,000-null confirmation of each corner's maximum (uniform and normal core);
- 20,000-null confirmation of the worst mixture within a contamination bound.

**Binary risk difference.** Certified exact size: decision tables over all outcome tables, plus the certified nuisance bound.

**Reproduction.** Commands are in BUILD-NOTES.md. Compute: about 3.2 CPU-hours for the main grid plus the adversarial phases, at nice 15 with 2 workers.
